Availability Watch — who is out, who is missing, and what we still cannot say

A dated read of the official Week 10 injury hub, transcribed player by player on 13 November 2026, plus the movement since the previous read. This is the input the brief asks for: it tracks injuries, it states which clubs are missing from the record, and it says plainly which half of the requirement — "defenders and receivers replaced by 2nd, 3rd or 4th string" — the league's own pages cannot answer.

Read at 2026-11-13T12:45Z from nfl.com/injuries. 28 clubs on the Week 10 slate, 296 player rows, 9 game-status designations.

What this page found that the site did not have yesterday

Three things changed at the source, and all three matter more than any single number on the page:

  • Every club on the slate now has a stated injury state. Yesterday the site published 24 of 28 coverage — SEA and CIN were absent from our file entirely, which FL-24 recorded as a collection gap. The official hub now carries full blocks for both. The gap closed at the source, not by us filling it in, and this snapshot transcribes 28 of 28.
  • Three more players have an official game status. There were 6 designation cells in the register; there are now 9. The three new ones are Detroit's — Kerby Joseph, Cade Mays and D.J. Reed, all OUT. This is the first week where a full defensive backfield and a starting centre can be priced as unavailable rather than as questionable.
  • The hub is a series, and it only grew. 17 players were added across 10 clubs and none were removed. Of the 277 players present in both reads, 49 changed practice code — 37 upgraded, 12 downgraded. A model that read yesterday's file and stopped would have been wrong about 66 of 296 rows without knowing it.

The last two columns are deliberately blank: no official public depth chart exists (FL-06) and no replacement effect has ever been measured on this site (gate AW-4). LAC and BAL are the two clubs the league answers with "No Injuries Reported" — a stated state, rendered as such, never as zero (S-19).

    Read the two blank columns as the answer to the hardest part of the brief. The requirement is to know when a 2nd, 3rd or 4th-string backup is replacing a starter, on offence and defence. The hub tells us, per player, whether he practised and whether he is out. It does not tell us where he sits on the depth chart, and no official public depth chart exists — nfl.com/teams/<club>/depth-chart/ returns the league's 404 page. Four clubs publish an unofficial chart and those are transcribed on the Depth & Deficiencies page. For the other 24 clubs, the honest answer is a blank, and filling it from a roster list would be inference, which the brief forbids.

      What this page does not do

      • It does not grade a strategy. No availability signal here has been tested against a result. The leaderboard is still empty for exactly that reason (Q-33, S-44).
      • It does not claim the snapshot is live. It is one read at one moment, with that moment printed at the top. The hub will move again before kickoff — the sedimentation problem is the whole point of S-35 and S-43.
      • It does not name who replaces whom. That is the blank column above, and it is blocked by FL-06, not by a lack of will.
      • It does not use a non-official source. Every row links nfl.com/injuries/. Player-level data from a betting operator or an aggregator does not appear anywhere in this dataset, per S-14.
