Proposals for review — nothing on this page is implemented

A working list of improvements, each with what it would fix, what it would let this site claim that it cannot claim today, what it costs, how it would be validated, and what could go wrong. Suggestions are reviewed before implementation, not after. 12 are listed; none is built.

Nothing on this page is implemented. Each suggestion states what it would fix, what it costs, what it would let the site claim that it cannot claim today, and what could go wrong. A suggestion moves to 'Approved' only on an explicit decision; the site never ships an unapproved idea as if it were a finding.

Decision rule. Approve on merit, not on enthusiasm. A suggestion that cannot be validated with the data already on this site should be DEFERRED, not built.

P-01Scrape per-game player box scores to unlock player-prop research
Problem: FL-09 and Q-13. The site can describe player props but cannot measure a single correlation, because the dataset contains season aggregates only. Every player-prop claim is therefore qualitative.
Proposal: For each of the 122 completed games, fetch the NFL.com gamebook/box-score page and store per-player passing, rushing, receiving and defensive lines keyed by game id. That immediately enables (a) player-vs-club-total dependence, (b) same-game-parlay leg correlation, (c) player prop hit rates against any threshold, (d) week-to-week variance and therefore real prop pricing.
PROPOSEDHighVery highValidated by: Cross-check each game's summed player yards against the game's team totals already in the dataset — the same equality rule that proved the 8 passing lines. Any gamebook that fails is discarded rather than reconciled.
Risk: NFL.com box scores may render without headers, exactly like the rushing and receiving leaderboards that failed in FL-08. If so, this suggestion dead-ends the same way and must be reported, not forced.
Unlocks: Player-prop combos, prop correlation, hit-rate tables, variance. The single largest capability gain available.		register ↗
P-02Transcribe the remaining 28 club depth charts
Problem: Q-05. Starter-versus-backup attribution exists for 4 of 32 clubs. 203 of 254 injury rows carry no depth position.
Proposal: Read each club's own `/team/depth-chart` page and add it to `data/depth_charts.json` in the same shape as the four that exist. Re-run `scripts/personnel.py` and `scripts/verify_personnel.py`.
PROPOSEDMediumHighValidated by: The existing nine personnel checks already run against whatever is in the file; they will catch duplicate position labels, a player at two depths of one group, and any depth asserted for a club without a chart.
Risk: Every club labels its chart 'Unofficial'. Two clubs may mean different things by a row. The check that a player never appears at two depths of one group catches transcription slips, not semantic drift.
Unlocks: League-wide deficiency table. Today the deficiency index is published for 4 clubs and blank for 28.		register ↗
P-03Widen the walk-forward fold to weeks 1–9 and re-grade the strategies
Problem: Q-10. Every strategy fails gate G2 (n ≥ 200) and the whole leaderboard reads UNDERPOWERED.
Proposal: Hold out at prediction time strictly what was known before kickoff, but start grading from week 1 with a minimum two-week burn-in for the ratings. That roughly doubles the leg count per strategy from 72 to about 144 for a 122-game season.
PROPOSEDLowMediumValidated by: The gate harness is unchanged; only the fold window moves. Nothing about the pass/fail rule is negotiated.
Risk: A shorter burn-in makes early-week ratings noisier, which can flatter or punish a strategy for the wrong reason. This must be published alongside the result, and the folded-in games must be re-checked against the log.
Unlocks: Nothing on its own. It moves the leaderboard closer to being decidable and makes the power statement honest rather than merely short.		register ↗
P-04Add a second, independent valuation of the passing/rushing split
Problem: FL-05. Kansas City's club page reports 3,069 total offensive yards while its own team-stat components sum to 3,139 — a 70-yard gap that is currently unexplained.
Proposal: Take total offensive yards from a second official presentation (the club statistics page) and compare it to passing-plus-rushing from the team-stat pages for all 32 clubs instead of just Kansas City. If the gap is systematic, it will show up in the residuals; if it is a one-club typo, that also becomes visible.
PROPOSEDLowMediumValidated by: A distribution of residuals. A single outlier is a typo; a consistent bias is a definitional difference and must be documented as such.
Risk: Two official presentations may simply define 'total offence' differently — for instance including or excluding sack yardage. That would be a labelling problem rather than a data error, and the site should say which.
Unlocks: Either closes FL-05 or converts it into a documented definitional note.		register ↗
P-05Build a cross-season baseline so the 2026 quirks can be tested
Problem: IR-04. Home-field advantage measures +0.00 points in 2026, which is so far from the conventional prior that it may be a one-season artefact rather than a real finding.
Proposal: Add the same verified game log for 2023, 2024 and 2025 and recompute HFA, margin standard deviation and the Pythagorean exponent on roughly 850 games instead of 122.
PROPOSEDMediumHighValidated by: The same 10 hard checks applied per season; a season that does not reproduce its own standings is excluded.
Risk: Older pages may be less stable to read. If a season cannot be verified to the same standard it must not be pooled in.
Unlocks: A defensible HFA prior, a real margin distribution, and enough sample to discuss the strategy gates at all.		register ↗
P-06Add market prices so that profitability can actually be tested
Problem: FL-01. No official odds source exists, so every strategy result is a hit-rate and a Brier score, never a return. The site cannot say whether any strategy makes money.
Proposal: Ingest an odds archive with a timestamp at or before kickoff, join it to the game log, and report closing-line value and return per leg alongside hit rate.
DEFERREDHighVery highValidated by: A line is only usable if its timestamp precedes kickoff. Any line without a timestamp is discarded.
Risk: This is where most analytics projects quietly start inventing numbers. It stays DEFERRED until a legitimate, timestamped source exists.
Unlocks: ROI, CLV, and a real answer to Q-09. Nothing smaller.		register ↗
P-07Publish an explicit survivorship and selection-bias note on the leaderboard
Problem: The strategy leaderboard shows six strategies, all underpowered. A reader can still mentally rank them and act on the top row.
Proposal: Put the sample size, the number of gates passed and a plain-language 'this cannot yet be distinguished from luck' badge on every row, and sort the table by gates passed rather than by hit rate.
PROPOSEDLowMediumValidated by: The badges must be generated from `analysis.json`, not typed by hand, so they cannot drift from the underlying result.
Risk: Badges can become decoration. They should be tied to the same gate objects the model emits.
Unlocks: Removes the main way this site could mislead someone — by ranking noise.		register ↗
P-08Model the deficiency index against results once depth coverage is real
Problem: The personnel page publishes a weighted deficiency index that is explicitly not a points estimate. That is honest but useless for prediction.
Proposal: Once P-02 lands, regress margin on the opponent's and own side's deficiency index with a ratings control. Publish the coefficient with a confidence interval, or publish that it is indistinguishable from zero.
PROPOSEDMediumMediumValidated by: Out-of-sample: fit on weeks 1–6, test on 7–9. The same walk-forward discipline as the strategies.
Risk: Depth charts lag coaching decisions and every chart is labelled unofficial, so measurement error is large and will bias the coefficient toward zero.
Unlocks: Answers Q-06 with a number instead of an index.		register ↗
P-09Reconstruct the rushing and receiving leaderboards by inferring column order
Problem: It looked like a quick win: two tables, 25 rows, obvious columns.
Proposal: Do not do this. It was attempted. Every arithmetic identity failed for every row under every candidate ordering — published yards per carry disagrees with yards divided by carries (Bijan Robinson 5.2 vs 5.0, Kenneth Walker 5.5 vs 7.0, Jahmyr Gibbs 4.8 vs 9.0), and yards per reception and yards per game fail the same way.
REJECTED_PREVIOUSLY——Validated by: Failed at the identity step, which is exactly what the identity step is for.
Risk: The tempting failure mode is to pick the ordering that 'looks about right' and publish it. That is how a data project starts lying.
Unlocks: Nothing. Superseded by P-01, which would source per-player pages with labelled headers instead.		register ↗
P-10Make the build refuse to publish when verification regresses
Problem: Verification currently runs when someone remembers to run it. A future edit could break a check and still go live.
Proposal: Add a pre-merge step that runs `verify_data.py`, `verify_personnel.py` and `analyse.py`, and blocks the merge if any hard check fails.
PROPOSEDLowMediumValidated by: Deliberately break a value in a branch and confirm the merge is blocked.
Risk: A flaky check will get disabled by whoever it blocks. Only hard checks may block; soft checks should warn.
Unlocks: The guarantee that what is live is what was verified.		register ↗
P-11State the model's confidence honestly at every table
Problem: A correlation of +0.876 on 32 clubs looks far more precise than it is.
Proposal: Show n on every correlation and add the caveat that 32 clubs is a small sample with no correction for the number of metrics tested, so the strongest correlations are partly selection.
PROPOSEDLowLowValidated by: Every correlation table must print its own n.
Risk: None beyond clutter.
Unlocks: Prevents the most common misinterpretation of this kind of page.		register ↗
P-12Add snap counts to measure how much a starter's absence actually matters
Problem: A starter listed on a depth chart may play 95% of snaps or 55%. The chart cannot tell you which, so 'starter' is a coarse label.
Proposal: Ingest snap-count data and weight the deficiency index by the missing player's share of his unit's snaps over the preceding three weeks.
DEFERREDHighMediumValidated by: Snap shares must sum to the unit's snap total per game.
Risk: Same licensing problem as P-06.
Unlocks: Turns 'starter out' into 'X% of the unit's workload out'.		register ↗

How to react to a suggestion

Approve

It becomes a task on the coordination board with a file scope, and it is implemented with the same verification standard as everything else. Approval is a decision, not an implication.

Defer

Kept on the list with the reason it is blocked. A deferred idea is not a rejected idea: several of these are waiting on a legitimate data source rather than on effort.

Reject

Recorded with the reason, so it is not proposed again. One entry (P-09) is already in this state because it was attempted and failed.

One suggestion is a warning, not an opportunity. P-09 is listed so that nobody repeats it: reconstructing the rushing and receiving leaderboards by inference was tried, every identity failed, and the correct response was to withhold the tables rather than pick the ordering that looked plausible.
