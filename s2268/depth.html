What a deficiency is here. Two official documents have to agree before this build will assert one. The league's own injury hub says who is unavailable; the club's own published depth chart says which string that player is and who is printed behind him. Where either document is silent the cell is not published — no inference from salary, draft position, jersey number or past starts.

Read the caveat first

Both clubs label the page this comes from an unofficial depth chart — Patriots.com states plainly that it “reflects the opinions of the Patriots.com writers,” and the Lions publish theirs as “compiled by Lions Football Communications.” They are official club publications, they are the only published statement of string order, and they are not the league's own depth chart, because the league does not publish one. That distinction is recorded as FL-2268-04 and it is the reason every row below cites the club page it came from.

Why these two clubs

Of the fourteen Week 10 games, exactly one has not been played and has filed game-status designations: the Munich game, NE @ DET, Sunday 15 November, 09:30 EST. It carries six of the seven designations on the whole Week 10 hub. The other clubs file practice participation but no status, so a “who is out” chain cannot be asserted for them yet. The remaining 24 clubs are the obvious next task and the URL pattern is in the source registry.

The chain that matters

New England's running-back room is the case the whole register exists for. Rhamondre Stevenson is the 1st string and is limited. TreVeyon Henderson is the 2nd string and is ruled out. So the first available replacement is the 3rd string, Corey Kiner, and behind him the 4th string, Lan Larison. That is exactly the second-, third- and fourth-string cascade the brief asks the system to know, and it comes from two official pages agreeing, not from a model.
