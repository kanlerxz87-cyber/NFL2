Leaderboard & rewards

Nothing on this leaderboard is a betting record. There is no price feed in this repository, so there is no closing line, no vig, no CLV and no return on investment. The competition ranks selection skill, which is measurable without a price, and it leaves every economics cell blank. The moment a price exists, the ROI column can be filled; until then, filling it would be invention.

The result

Ten candidates were pre-registered — eight real signals and two deliberate negative controls — then scored on 72 holdout games (weeks 5–9) that no fitted coefficient ever saw. 596 decisions were settled. 4 candidates survive a Benjamini–Hochberg correction at a 10% false-discovery rate; two of those are the negative controls doing their job in the wrong direction or the right one.

Ranked by log loss, a proper scoring rule: it rewards being right and penalises being confidently wrong, so a lucky streak on few bets cannot top the table. The rank column reads “unranked” when no candidate clears the luck gate, because ranking noise is how a competition talks itself into a bad strategy.

How to read the hit rates

The favourite signal wins 45 of 66 decisions, 68.2%, with an exact binomial p of 0.0043. Every fibre of a betting brain wants to call that an edge. It is not one: a team with the better season point differential is the favourite, and winning two times in three is what being a favourite means. Whether that was already in the price is unknowable here, because there is no price. The one number that would settle it — the closing line — is the one number this repository does not have.

The contrarian control wins 21 of 66 on the same games, which is the exact complement. That is the point of including it: it prices the multiple-comparisons problem by showing what the mirror image of a real signal looks like to the same test.

P-PYTH Pythagorean favourite pick moneyline side level Select the team with the higher Pythagorean expectation, exponent 2.37, from season-to-date points for and against. Abstain when the two expectations are within 0.020 of each other. The same idea with the blowout noise damped. official source
P-L3 Last-three momentum pick moneyline side momentum Select the team with the better point differential over its last three games. Abstain if either team has fewer than three games played or the two are exactly equal. Short-window recency. Expected to be the noisiest real signal here. official source
P-REST Rest advantage pick moneyline side scheduling Select the team with more days since its previous game. Abstain unless the difference in rest is at least two days. Selective by construction: it only fires on bye and short-week mismatches. official source
P-CONTRA Contrarian dog pick moneyline side level Select the team with the WORSE season-to-date point differential per game. Abstain when the two rates are within 0.50 points of each other. Second negative control. It exists to price the multiple-comparisons problem. official source
P-SPREAD-MODEL Our originated spread pick moneyline side model Select the side our own fitted spread model favours (the sign of the predicted home margin). Abstain when the absolute predicted home margin is below 1.00 point. Uses the coefficients fitted on weeks 2-4 only. official source
P-FAV-DOG Home unless clearly worse pick moneyline side composite Select the home team unless the away team's point differential per game is better by more than 7.00 points. Abstain on neutral-site games. Home field plus a threshold, i.e. the simplest version of a real handicap model. official source
P-SOS Schedule-adjusted differential pick moneyline side level Select the team with the higher point differential per game after subtracting the average point differential per game of the opponents it has faced. Abstain when the two adjusted rates are within 0.50 points of each other. Strength of schedule, one iteration, no convergence. official source
