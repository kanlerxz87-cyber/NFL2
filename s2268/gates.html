Validation gates

Ten gates: four that stop us cheating ourselves, six that stop us betting.

On this pageGate boardThe blocking chainHow these differ
Ten gates. Four pass, six block. The four that pass are the ones that stop us cheating ourselves — point-in-time features, a clean fit/test split, a pre-registration hash, a minimum sample. The six that block are the ones that stop us betting: no price feed, no measured prop correlations, no forward paper track record, no elapsed seasons.

“Hard” means the gate cannot be waived by a good story. A blocked gate is not a defect in a strategy; it is a missing input, and each row names it.

The blocking chain, in order

  1. G-C7, price feed. Nothing that involves money can be evaluated without one. Until a licensed feed exists and every price carries a capture timestamp, ROI, CLV and units stay not published. This is the single decision that unblocks the most: Q-01.
  2. G-C8, leg correlation. Game-level legs are measured on the correlations page. Player-prop legs are not, because no prop-line archive exists. Any same-game product involving a prop stays unpriced.
  3. G-C9, forward paper tracking. One Week 10 game has settled. Fifty forward-tracked paper bets with decision timestamps are needed before anything is promoted past T0.
  4. G-C10, two seasons. Nine weeks of one season exist. This gate cannot be argued with, only outlasted.

How these differ from the root register's gates

The root validation gates page carries fourteen gates, all “blocked” or “not started”, because each one describes an input the repository does not yet have. These ten are the same idea applied to something that does exist: a frozen corpus and a scoring harness. Four of them can be evaluated today, and they pass. The point of the split is that a gate board on which nothing can ever pass is not a gate board, it is a wish list.
