Parlays & prop combos

Disjoint parlay construction, measured against the independence assumption, with the economics left blank where they need a price.

On this pageWhy construction mattersResultsThe economics
Parlays are built disjoint: inside a week, the games a strategy bet are taken in corpus order and grouped into consecutive pairs or triples that share no game. That makes each parlay an independent observation, so the binomial test against the independent-product rate is actually valid. The overlapping enumeration a careless build would use is counted at the bottom of the table and labelled for what it is.

Why the construction matters more than the result

Cross every pair of games in a week and a strategy with eleven bets per week yields several hundred parlays from six weeks of football. Each game then appears in dozens of them. Any p value computed on that set treats the same twelve games as fresh evidence every time, and will manufacture significance out of nothing. The disjoint construction throws most of that away and keeps only parlays that share no game — about thirty of them, which is a small honest sample instead of a large dishonest one.

What we found

Across every disjoint two-leg and three-leg parlay in the table, not one deviates significantly from what independence would predict. The smallest p value on the page is 0.1047, on 31 parlays. The honest conclusion is not “parlays are independent”; it is that with about thirty parlays we cannot detect the correlation that certainly exists. That is a sample-size statement, and it is the same wall the rest of this project keeps hitting.

Legs are single-game side selections. A parlay never repeats a game: two legs on the same game are one bet, not two, and the correlation table on the correlations page shows how close to one bet they really are.

The economics, without a price

A two-leg parlay at standard -110 per leg pays about 2.6 to 1, which needs a 27.7% hit rate to break even. Two independent 55% legs give 30.3%; two independent 50% legs give 25.0%. Our best genuine signal runs at 68.2% per leg, which would make a two-leg parlay enormously profitable — if the market priced those legs at even money, which it does not and would not.

Without a closing price there is no way to know what a book would actually pay for these legs, so no parlay return is published anywhere in this tree. What is published is the part that does not need a price: the hit rates, the correlations, and the sample size you would need before believing any of it.
