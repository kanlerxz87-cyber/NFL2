The headline is that the 2026 rule lets any club declare an onside kick at any time, leading or trailing — and that the third-party summaries of that rule contradict each other, which is exactly why this register reads the league's own pages and nothing else.

Why a "regime prior" and not a rule summary

A total is a forecast of possessions and field position. The kickoff rules set the starting field position of every possession that follows a score, and the onside-kick rules decide whether a trailing club can manufacture an extra one. Those are not small effects, and they changed three years running:

SeasonWho may declare an onside kickWhen
2024trailing clubfourth quarter only
2025trailing clubat any point
2026any clubat any point

A total model fitted on 2023–2025 data is fitted on a world where the leading club could not trade field position for a possession. That assumption expired in March 2026.

The disagreement this settles

Search for the 2026 onside-kick rule and you will find both answers, confidently stated. Several third-party summaries say the change lets a trailing club declare at any point; others say any club can. The league's own pages are unambiguous:

  • The official 2026 NFL Rulebook lists
  • 6-1-6: "Permits the kicking team to declare an onside kick at any time during the game."
  • NFL.com's own report of the vote adds the clause that removes the ambiguity: the change applies
  • "regardless of the score. Previously, only the trailing team could attempt an onside kick."

    • Neither says "trailing". The register uses both official sources and no others.

    One thing did not change, and it is the reason the effect on totals is bounded: the kick must still be declared to the referee, who tells the receiving team. A surprise onside kick remains impossible, so the receiving club always sets its recovery formation and the recovery rate stays low. The register therefore does not publish a recovery-rate figure. Widely quoted single-digit percentages for the dynamic-kickoff era could not be traced to an official league page from this session, and a number this project cannot source is a number this project leaves blank.

    What a model has to do differently

    • Comeback curves. A fourth-quarter win-probability curve built before 2026 is built on the
    • assumption that only the trailing club can manufacture a possession. Both clubs can now, so the
    • possession-count distribution that drives the curve has a fatter tail on both sides of the score.
    • Field position after a penalty-moved kickoff. With the touchback from the 50 now spotted at the 20,
    • the expected starting field position after a penalty-forced kickoff changes, and with it the
    • expected-points value of that kickoff. A total fitted on the old 25/35 split is mispriced on exactly the
    • plays that follow a personal foul.
    • Return rates. The setup zone moved from a 6-3-2 to a 5-4-2 alignment. Touchback rate and average
    • return start are not comparable to 2025 without re-measuring, so a 2025-based return-rate prior should
    • be treated as stale rather than carried forward.
    • Late-game ejections. Replay can now put a flag down for a flagrant act the on-field crew missed, so
    • a starter can be removed during the closing drive on a path that pre-2026 models never priced.

      • What is deliberately not here

      • No success-rate prior. Not published by an official source, so it stays blank.
      • No roof or surface. That is BL-59 and it stays open; see the
      • fixture venues page for the published venue register.
      • No claim that this regime has been measured. These are priors to pre-register, not findings. The
      • pre-registration lock is the mechanism that keeps it that way.
