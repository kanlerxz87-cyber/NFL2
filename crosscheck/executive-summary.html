2. The one number that changes how you read this site

The repository's Week 10 injury figures were captured at 04:45 UTC. The same page, re-read at 22:35 UTC, carried 25 more players and had 4 changed participation cells. Two clubs that read "No Injuries Reported" in the morning — Seattle and Cincinnati — publish full blocks in the evening. Nothing was mis-transcribed. The snapshot simply went stale inside one day, and no page in the repository carried the capture time prominently enough for a reader to notice.

That is why the first suggestion is not a model. It is a timestamp that the build refuses to omit.

3. What the repository can already prove

  • Points are a closed system. Adding the 32 clubs' points for gives 6,246; points against gives 6,246. The game log reconciles to the standings on record, points and byes.
  • Home-field advantage is not visible in this season's outcomes: 67 home wins, 68 away wins, 1 tie across 136 games.
  • Team scores are close to independent within a game: home versus away points correlates at +0.076 over 136 games, recomputed here to six decimals and identical to the value published by another tree.
  • All 6 published game-level correlation coefficients recompute exactly from the committed game log. That is the strongest form of verification in the repository: an outsider can repeat it.

4. What the repository cannot prove, and should stop implying

  • Any edge over a price. No price is committed. The one backtest comparison that exists cannot be reproduced from the repository.
  • Any depth rank. No league-wide depth chart exists. Club sites cover roughly a third of the league.
  • Any prop or parlay correlation. Team-level outcome correlation is measurable; player-level and same-game pricing need data that is not here.
  • Any ranked strategy. Zero settled bets exist across every strategy in every tree.
