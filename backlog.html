What this page is, and what it is not

data/requirements.csv holds 46 requirements. They were reconstructed from the summary of the brief. The brief itself also contains a line-item backlog of specific directives — a price-snapshot store, a verified results feed, side-then-total-then-moneyline ordering, depth-of-replacement rather than fact-of-replacement, injury as a surprise rather than a level, a 32-team roster and IR sweep, a point-in-time snapshot ledger, a stadium weather overlay, a two-stage skill leaderboard, three explicit Reject: decisions, and twenty-eight items tagged with VR- and VQ- codes.

None of those 71 lines are tracked anywhere else in the repository. grep -rl "VR-24" . and grep -rl "price-snapshot" . both return no hits outside .git. They are transcribed here so that "collect, organize and analyze utilizing a full list of requirements" has a list to be checked against.

Each row carries the gate that blocks it and the number that would clear it, using the same fourteen gates as the validation gate register.

Source class. The directives, the gate mappings and the clearing numbers are owner instruction and project design. They are not official NFL facts and are not verified by an NFL source. The Source column on each row links the official page that would have to be read to satisfy the directive — that link is an official source, and it is the dependency, not evidence that the work is done. Statuses, gate mappings and clearing numbers are project design. re-read 2026-11-15 means an agent fetched that page on that date and recorded what it showed; not re-read today is a real value and is not a claim; no official source exists means the league does not publish the thing the directive needs.
Two registers, one brief — read them together. While this page was being built, a parallel session merged a Brief Coverage & Freshness page with an 84-clause matrix over the same brief. They answer different questions and neither is a superset: that page classes each clause as covered, partial, blocked, uncovered or resolved; this page names the gate that blocks it, the number that would clear it, and the official page that would have to be read. The Coverage clause column on every row below is the id of the nearest clause in that matrix, matched by string similarity at a 0.72 cutoff by tools/build_backlog_58e9c708.py — it is a navigation aid computed by a script, not an official fact and not a claim that the two rows mean exactly the same thing. 7 of the 71 rows have no clause near enough to link, and are shown as not published rather than linked to something wrong.
