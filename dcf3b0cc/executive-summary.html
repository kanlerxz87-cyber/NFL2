Executive summary

Session dcf3b0cc · official NFL injury hub read 2026-11-15T12:55Z · written for a reader who has to decide what to do next, not for a reader who wants to check the arithmetic. Every number below is counted from the sources named on the method page.

What was done

The official injury hub was read end to end today and every published row for the Week 10 slate was transcribed verbatim: 347 rows across 28 clubs and 14 fixtures, each with the practice status, the game status and the official player link the page prints. That capture was then compared, row by row, against the Week 10 injury table the repository already publishes (read 2026-11-13T12:46Z from the same URL), and joined to the club depth charts transcribed on 2026-11-13 to produce an offence-versus-defence deficiency matrix.

The five things that matter

  1. The table the site publishes is missing almost every designation. It carries 9 game-status designations across 296 rows; the same page read today carries 171 — 70 Out, 11 Doubtful, 90 Questionable. It also leaves 287 of 296 injury-description cells blank. Any count built on that table is counting a page that no longer exists.
  2. One of the fourteen fixtures has no injury coverage at all. Neither BAL nor LAC appears anywhere in the published table, while the page lists 29 rows for them today. A seventh of the slate is invisible to any count built on that table.
  3. Two days is enough to change two thirds of the table. Of the 296 rows both reads share, 102 are identical and 194 differ; 55 differ on practice status alone. A single weekly snapshot cannot see the Wednesday-to-Friday movement the brief asks us to measure.
  4. The deficiency feature can only name a replacement for 284 of 347 listed players (81.8%), and for 55 of the 70 players ruled Out. HOU resolves none of its listed players at all, because its chart is published as an image.
  5. Nothing correlates yet, and that is the honest answer. Seven descriptive correlations over the 28 clubs playing this week, both inputs officially sourced: none significant at 5%, largest absolute r under 0.28. One week of 28 clubs cannot separate injuries from roster quality.

What to do next, in order

  1. Label every injury table on the canonical site with the time it was read, and keep both reads published. This costs nothing and removes the largest source of silent error.
  2. Adopt the schedule-page order as the single game-id convention, or drop game_id as a join key.
  3. Add a drift alarm that fails the build when a club that is playing publishes zero rows.
  4. Capture reserve lists, then game-day inactives and snap counts — in that order. The second is what turns a suspected replacement into a confirmed one.
  5. Only then pre-register the personnel feature and test it as an ablation, not as a fixed penalty.
