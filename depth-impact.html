Depth & Deficiencies

Who is replacing whom because of a confirmed absence — and, as important, what we cannot yet quantify about it.
On this pageConfirmed Week 10 depth and availability eventsWhat the injury and depth data already establishes for Week 10Who is actually playing instead: 18 measured cases, one of them a vacancyThe framework that replaces the missing depth chart

The brief asked the system to know when second, third and fourth-string backups have to replace starters, on both sides of the ball. Two facts shape this page. First, the league publishes no depth chart — the expected official path is an error page, so depth rank cannot be looked up, only reconstructed (FL-06). Second, the hub's injury table is a practice document, so the reliable evidence for "a starter is unavailable" lives in the roster-move and designation stream, which is what this page ingests.

What we can therefore state today, verified: confirmed rule-outs, injured-reserve placements, designated-to-return events, practice participation, and coach-announced starting changes. What we deliberately do not state: which depth-chart rung the replacement occupies, and what the replacement costs in points or yards. Those columns are absent from this page rather than guessed — the effect estimate needs playing-time data we are not entitled to store yet (Q-02).

Who is actually playing instead: 18 measured cases, one of them a vacancy

This is the direct answer to "tell me when a 2nd, 3rd or 4th string is replacing a starter", and it is narrower than the requirement asks for. Four clubs — KC, GB, NE and DET — publish a depth chart that has been transcribed and checked here. For those four clubs the join is exact: a name match inside a club's own chart, against the same club's rows in the official practice report. Nothing is inferred across sources.

The rule, stated so it can be argued with: a player is available if the official report lists him as a full participant, or does not list him at all (the report covers players with a reportable condition, so absence from it is not an injury claim). A limited or did not practice player is treated as not available to start, which is deliberately conservative — a limited participant often does play.

Two more are worth naming because they are one injury away from a cascade: DET CB_1 and GB RB each have a four-deep chain where only the starter is down, so the backup is a genuine 2nd string and not a patchwork. A pricing model should treat a four-deep chain with a healthy 2nd string differently from a two-deep chain with a limited one, and that distinction is now measurable rather than asserted.

The vacancy, which is worse than a third stringer

One case does not fit the "next man up" shape at all. New England at left guard: the starter Ben Brown did not practise and is listed Out, and the 2nd-string Walter Rouse also did not practise. Nobody on the published two-deep is available. That is a vacancy, not a third-string replacement, and the first version of this table silently dropped it because the loop only recorded cases where someone was available.

It is published now, marked vacant, with the next-man-up column deliberately empty — filling in a name there would be inventing a lineup the club has not published. This is the single most decision-relevant row on the page: a guard vacancy on an offensive line is exactly the situation where a totals model should widen its distribution rather than shift its mean, and it is the kind of thing a depth chart exists to surface.
