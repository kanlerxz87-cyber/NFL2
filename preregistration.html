Pre-Registration Lock — Week 10

The first prediction this repository has ever pinned before an outcome existed: one rule, one threshold, fourteen fixtures, a SHA-256, and a grading tool that fails if the file is touched afterwards.
On this pageThe ledgerThe threshold was chosen once — and here is what choosing it differently would have doneWhat the one settled game says, and what it does notHow this becomes a leaderboard entry rather than a noteWhat is deliberately missing from this file

The rule, in one sentence. Our published number for a game is built from points scored and allowed through week 9, so it cannot see who is playing this week; when one club's availability load — designated out plus did not practise, counted off the league's own filing — is at least four players heavier than the opponent's, we lean the healthier club against our own line, and every other fixture is recorded as a no-call rather than dropped.

That is ST-15 on the strategy registry. It was written at 12:45 UTC on 13 November 2026, four hours before the earliest Week 10 kickoff (14:30 UTC, Munich), from a hub read taken in the same hour. The file data/prereg_wk10_66faefac.csv is hashed in data/prereg_wk10_66faefac.sha256, and python3 tools/verify_prereg_66faefac.py recomputes every derived cell from the inputs: an edit to a count, a threshold or a call now changes the hash and the check fails.

Load is designated-out plus did-not-practise players on the official hub, one per player. Differential is away minus home, so a positive number means the visitor is the more depleted club. Stake is zero for every row: gates G-01 (entitlement) and G-07 (vig and cost accounting) are not passed, so nothing here is a bet — it is a directional record against our own published number, which is the only number this repository can honestly be measured against without a price feed.

Two calls out of thirteen un-played fixtures — a 15% hit rate on the slate, which is what "selective" is supposed to mean, measured rather than asserted:

  • W10-06 Miami at Indianapolis. The Colts file a load of six — six non-participants and no designations, so nothing in that number is double-counted — against the Dolphins' two, so the lean is Miami. Both clubs are missing a quarterback in the wider sense: the league's own reporting has Miami placing Malik Willis on injured reserve, which removes him from the weekly report altogether — so the count says Indianapolis is the depleted club while the transactions say the opposite. That inversion is the blind spot Q-51 is about.
  • W10-11 Seattle at Las Vegas. Six Seahawks non-participants against a Raiders block of four rows and a load of zero, so the lean is the home side. Seattle's six non-participants include the quarterback the league's own reporting rules out (Darnold, with Drew Lock again starting), and the Raiders' entire block is four rows.
  • The fixture that got away, and the one that should worry us. San Francisco at Dallas sits at −3, one player inside the threshold, on a day when Dallas filed nine non-participants. Green Bay against Minnesota is the harder case: four 1st-string columns are empty for Green Bay, Minnesota files six non-participants, and the rule sees a +1 differential and passes. A body count cannot tell a starting running back from a fourth-string long snapper. That is the finding this experiment is designed to produce, and it is why S-62 proposes splitting the load by chart column.

    What the one settled game says, and what it does not

    Washington at New York finished before this rule existed, so it is not part of the locked sample. It is published anyway, because hiding it would be worse than showing it: the rule would have taken the home side (Washington filed five unavailable players, the Giants zero), the Giants lost by 3 against our published 2.5, and the call misses. One row. The settled page has the full four-way comparison for the same fixture, where our spread lost to a do-nothing season mean by 2.5 points.

    The arithmetic on required sample is already on this site and does not move for anyone: detecting a 1.0-point difference in spread error at 80% power and a 5% level needs 1,382 games off the 136-game verified corpus, and gate G-08 sets n ≥ 400 settled bets before any strategy claim. Thirteen fixtures is 1% of the first figure; the two locked calls are half a percent of the 400 the gate demands. A 2-of-2 start would be luck; a 0-of-2 start would be luck; the number of bets per week this rule allows is the only thing that is real information here.

    How this becomes a leaderboard entry rather than a note

    1. The games settle on 15–16 November. Rows are appended to data/games_2026_wk10_played.csv — never to the frozen weeks 1–9 corpus, which S-26 pins by hash.
    2. python3 tools/compute_settled_wk10.py && python3 tools/compute_availability_66faefac.py && python3 tools/build_site.py re-scores the settled page and re-emits the pre-registration, which must produce a byte-identical locked file; tools/verify_prereg_66faefac.py then prints hits and misses against the official margin.
    3. If the file re-hashes differently, the lock is broken and the strategy does not move up a tier — that is gate G-11 doing its job, not a build failure to route around.
    4. Rewards stay unallocated through this whole sequence. The leaderboard rules pay on measured CLV and settled volume, and neither exists while the price feeds in Q-01 are unlicensed. An entry with locked calls and no settled bets sits at T0, which is where the tier table says it belongs.

      5. What is deliberately missing from this file

      • No point margin is claimed. The lean is a side, not a number of points. Any conversion from "four unavailable bodies" to "points" would need a positional value that no official source publishes — see the fair-line lab, where the same restraint leaves the weighting columns blank.
      • No correlation adjustment. These are single legs, one per fixture, so the parlay coupling questions on the correlation page do not arise; a same-game second leg would have to clear gate G-05 before it could be added to a locked row.
      • No live price. The comparison beside our number is a column in a vendored community archive whose open-or-close vintage has never been documented (FL-30), not a closing price captured at decision time. The whole story is on the price archive page, and it is why the stake column reads zero.
