The correlation values the brief asks for, computed on games this repository can actually verify — with intervals wide enough to be true, and an explicit statement of what a number this small cannot support.

The design page (Correlation) lists twelve pair classes and publishes no rho, because fitting one requires joint-outcome data nobody had assembled here. This page is that assembly, done from the one corpus in the repository that survives a row-by-row source check: the 136 completed 2026 games, each linked to its official nfl.com game page. Nothing here is fitted on a model's own output except where the row says so.

What each one does to the decision

M-01 · the two teams' scores barely move together (rho 0.076, CI −0.088 to 0.244). A "both teams to score over" style stack therefore behaves close to independently, and the independence assumption in Bet types is not obviously wrong for this pair. Note the interval, not the point estimate: it is compatible with anything from a mild negative to a real positive.

M-03 · last week's blowout tells you almost nothing about next week (rho 0.088 on 240 consecutive club-game pairs, CI −0.03 to 0.20). This is the removal of luck the brief asks for, stated as a number: at club-game level the week-to-week signal is ~1% of shared variance. A strategy whose whole idea is "team X dominated, back them again" is buying noise at full price, and the leaderboard should not reward it (S-38).

M-04 · record tracks Pythagorean expectation tightly (rho 0.863, CI 0.741 to 0.938). The residual is the interesting part: the wins a club took that its point margin did not predict. Two clubs rest, one of them plays, and the margin moves by around 12 points in the rested club's direction on 17 games. That is implausibly large for a fatigue effect and entirely plausible for a selection effect: the bye falls differently for good and bad clubs, and a single season cannot separate the two. Treat as a hypothesis to test on 2013–2025, not a signal to trade.

M-06 · home-field advantage in 2026 is 0.50 points, CI −1.80 to +2.80 (129 non-neutral games). The most cited number in football betting is not measurable this season, and the conventional 2.5 points sits far outside this interval. Every model in this repository that hard-codes a home field is currently importing a prior, not a measurement — which is why the coefficient here is estimated rather than assumed, and why the Munich fixture is flagged instead of adjusted (FL-32).

Same-game parlay coupling — the number that changes a ticket

A two-leg same-game parlay is priced by multiplying two marginal probabilities. That is only fair if the legs are independent. Two legs a bettor stacks constantly — home covering the spread and the game going over — are negatively coupled, and the measured size of that coupling is the whole story:

Leg pairMeasured onJoint observedProduct of marginalsA ticket priced on independence is wrong by
home covers our spread + game over our total88 games, our own lines0.18180.207914.4% too generous in hit rate

Against the market's own lines the true joint hit rate is 0.1364 where the product of the two marginals implies 0.1942. A parlay ticket priced off independence therefore understates the true price the book is charging — the correlation is not a free lunch for the parlay bettor, it is a second vig. Row-level values are in the table above (p_a, p_b, joint_observed, joint_independent, price_error_pct).

The consequence for the competition: any parlay strategy must be scored on the correlation-adjusted joint, not on the legs individually, and the adjustment has to come from a joint history. Ours is one season long, so the SGP rule for now is S-40: price the coupling, cap the exposure at one ticket per game, and do not let a parlay strategy promote a tier on a sample this size.

Player-prop combos: still not measurable

Prop legs need per-player outcomes and a prop-line history. The league publishes neither, and this repository holds no prop archive — so the QB-completion × rushing-try couplings stay blank on the Correlation register, and not published here too. That is a data-entitlement decision (Q-02), not a modelling failure.
