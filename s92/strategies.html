The competition, as specified

Ten pre-registered strategies: five singles, three parlays, two player-prop combos. Each is entered against a gate set that a strategy must clear before it can be called skilled, and a second, softer set before it can be rewarded. Nothing here has been bet. The leaderboard is empty because no strategy has cleared the gates, and printing an empty leaderboard is more honest than printing a backtested one.

Two classes. Hard gates block promotion or the "skilled" label outright; soft gates only affect the reward tier.

Why eight of ten are blocked

Because the gates are ordered by provenance, not by significance. G-0 requires every input to carry a source and a licence position. No price feed exists, so any strategy whose entry rule mentions a price fails G-0 before any backtest is even run. That is the intended behaviour: it prevents a backtest on third-party data from being presented as a result.

What the first real strategy would look like

The first strategy that could clear the gates is not a parlay. It is a single-market strategy with a licensed price, a pre-registered rule, a stated sample target and a recorded first-bet timestamp — most likely an availability-surprise total, because that is where the repository's own data is strongest. Everything else waits on data.
