Correlation, measured only where joint data exists

The brief asks for correlation values, for single bets, parlays and player-prop combos. The honest answer is that only the game-level pairs are measurable from the repository's verified data. Anything involving players requires joint player-level outcomes, which we do not have. Those cells are blank.

These are descriptive statistics on one season. They are not estimates of a market relationship, and they cannot price a parlay on their own. A parlay price needs the joint probability of both legs winning, which needs the same-game joint distribution — not a correlation coefficient alone.

Removing luck

Four filters, all computable from the repository's own data.

1. Pythagorean luck residual

Each club's actual wins compared with a Pythagorean expectation, with the exponent printed as a convention (2.37) rather than a measured parameter. The largest residual in the league is Denver at +2.17 wins; the unluckiest is Tampa Bay at -2.17. Both are within about 1.5 standard deviations of expectation, which is the point: nobody in this league has been very lucky yet.

2. Split-half persistence

Point differential in weeks 1-4 compared with weeks 5-9: r = 0.186 across 32 clubs. Team quality repeats only weakly over nine weeks, which is why a nine-week model is not a basis for a skilled label.

3. One-score records

League-wide, one-score games are 76-76. Close-game records are a coin flip, and any strategy that depends on them is depending on variance.

4. Multiple-testing correction, pre-registered

Any strategy that is tested more than once must clear a Bonferroni-corrected threshold. The threshold is fixed in advance (S-103) so it cannot be chosen after seeing the result.

What this means for decision making

  • No club is a proven over- or under-performer on this sample. Every luck residual is inside the

range that random variation alone would produce.

  • No strategy can be certified from nine weeks. The sample gate (G-4) exists precisely for this.
  • The measured game-level correlations are small, which is good news for cross-game parlays (legs are

close to independent) and bad news for anyone hoping to find a large, simple correlation signal.

  • The prop question is unanswered, not answered negatively. Blank is not zero, and the route to an

answer is a participation-data licence, not a better formula.
