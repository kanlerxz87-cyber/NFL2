What these lines are

Thirteen Week 11 fair lines originated from the repository's verified 136-game log. They are not market lines and they are not betting advice. The market columns are blank because no price data exists in the repository; they will stay blank until a feed is licensed (Q-101).

The method is deliberately simple and reproducible:

  1. A margin rating per club, fitted by iterating team strengths until each club's average residual

margin is zero, then centring the ratings on the league.

  1. A scoring model per club: offence and defence ratings around the 2026 league average of 22.96 points

per team per game.

  1. Home-field advantage set to zero by published policy (S-109), because the 2026 estimate

(+0.50 points over 129 games) has an interval that comfortably includes zero.

  1. The neutral-site game (Minnesota at San Francisco, Mexico City) has no home term at all.

Why the totals look high

The fair totals are a direct consequence of 2026 scoring: the league is averaging 45.9 points a game, and a few clubs (Cincinnati, Jacksonville, Buffalo) have offence ratings far above that. A total of 56.9 for New Orleans at Chicago is what the model says, not what a book would say; a book would shade it down. The absence of a market line is the whole problem. Without one, we cannot tell whether a 56.9 is insight or error.

The backtest that keeps this honest

Same model, leave-one-week-out over the nine weeks we have.

`` Spread our MAE 10.55 zero-line baseline 10.62 95% interval on the difference [-1.23, 1.12] Total our MAE 12.86 league-average 11.62 95% interval on the difference [0.04, 2.52] Correlation of predicted and actual margin 0.331 Correlation of predicted and actual total 0.081 ``

Two things follow, and both are printed on the page rather than buried in a footnote:

  • The spread model is not yet better than a zero line. The difference interval spans zero.
  • The total model is worse than the league average. Its error is higher than assuming 45.9 every week.

A model that does not beat a naive baseline is not a signal, and no strategy built on top of it can pass the sample gate. The correct next step is more data and a market benchmark, not more model complexity.
