Executive summary

The repository holds one genuinely verified dataset (the 2026 standings, reconciled to the official league table with zero mismatches), one partially verified dataset (a 136-game game log whose aggregate reconciles exactly but whose individual scores cannot be re-read through this fetch path), one stale dataset (the injury table, a filing behind the official hub), and one absent dataset (prices, of any kind). Everything else is specification.

That ordering matters because it sets the order of work: collect better before modelling more. The three decisions that unblock the most are a price feed, a participation-data licence, and an agreement on what a depth slot means when the clubs themselves label their charts unofficial.

Read that honestly: the spread model's advantage is not distinguishable from zero, and the total model is worse than assuming the league average. That is the ordinary outcome for a first model with nine weeks of data and no market benchmark, and it is the reason no strategy is certified.

Availability and depth

12 starter lines across the three charted clubs show a starter who did not practise fully or was ruled out. Exactly one is a genuine cascade — the New England running-back line, where the starter did not practise and his listed backup was ruled out, so the next available body is the third name on the chart. That is the concrete form of "down to a third-stringer", and it is rare: one line in 68 charted lines at these three clubs.
