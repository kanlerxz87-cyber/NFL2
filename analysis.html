Are game totals and margins independent?

This is the whole basis for how parlays are treated on this site. If these were strongly related, combining legs would be a different problem entirely.

All four relationships are weak. That is a real result, not a null one: it justifies modelling winner and total separately.

Measured over 136 games: r(home score, away score) = +0.076. That means the two sides of a game are close to independent, so multiplying the leg probabilities is a defensible approximation for THIS season - but the estimate is weak and is not a licence to assume independence in general. The correlation is estimated on a single partial season, so it is reported with its n and treated as provisional rather than as a structural law.

The luck ladder

Pythagorean wins would be the club's record if it had converted its scoring margin at the league's normal rate. The gap between that and its actual record is the part of its season that did not come from scoring margin.

Most over-performing: Denver Broncos

7 wins against 4.83 expected. One-score games: 6-0. A club that wins every close game has usually been a little lucky rather than a little clutch.

Most under-performing: Tampa Bay Buccaneers

3 wins against 5.17 expected. One-score games: 1-6. The mirror image of the same effect.
