Strategy Leaderboard — Skill, Not Luck

A competition where strategies earn rewards for repeatable, validated edge — not for one hot week. Every strategy must pass the gates below before it can rank. Performance cells are blank until a strategy actually passes; we do not invent win rates.

How ranking works (proposed — see S-04): Rank by out-of-sample closing-line value (CLV) + ROI with minimum sample size, not by raw win rate. A strategy that beats the closing line consistently is showing skill; one that only wins in-sample is showing luck.

Correlation — measure it, don’t assume it

For any two signals X and Y (e.g. “team covers” and “total goes over”, or two prop legs), compute Pearson’s r over paired historical games:

r = Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ) / √[ Σ(x−x̄)² · Σ(y−ȳ)² ] |r| < 0.10 negligible · 0.10–0.30 weak · 0.30–0.50 moderate · > 0.50 strong High |r| ⇒ legs are not independent ⇒ parlay fair price must use the joint probability, not the product of marginals.

Correlation ≠ causation. A strong r between “QB throws a lot” and “WR catches a lot” does not prove either causes the other — both may be driven by game script (trailing). Always check n, out-of-sample stability, and the football mechanism before building a combo.

Removing luck — checklist for every submission

  • Report n, ROI, and 95% confidence interval — never win rate alone.
  • Show CLV vs the closing line; a strategy that cannot beat the close has not shown skill.
  • Split results by home/away, favorite/underdog, and month — flag any split that flips sign.
  • Disclose every parameter tuned and the exact test window. Undisclosed tuning = disqualified.
  • For parlays/props: publish leg correlations and the joint fair price, not just the ticket price.
