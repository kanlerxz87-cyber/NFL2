Independent Q51 audit and tested research engine: open the evidence dashboard. It publishes current-snapshot research lines, descriptive correlations, fail-closed gates and the full Flagged for Review register. Outputs are paper-only and unvalidated; no betting edge is claimed.

Read this first

This site has two kinds of pages and they never mix on one page without a divider:

  • Measured (green badge) — tables generated from data/*.csv, every row carrying a link to the official league page it was transcribed from.
  • Specified (blue badge) — the design of the pricing engine, signals, gates and competition rules. These are proposals, not results.
  • Decisions (amber badge) — the question register, the suggestion register and the review queue. Each suggestion is labeled as adopted, recommended, owner-only, or blocked; a recommendation is not an owner decision.

    • The root register remains design-only. Independent Q51 research outputs are labelled unvalidated and paper-only; no betting edge is claimed. Blank cells are rendered as not published rather than filled with an estimate — see verification method.

    The blocking chain

    Four items sit in front of almost everything else. They are decisions and access, not coding:

    1. Prices. Without an opening and closing price per market there is no vig to strip, no fair value to deviate from, no CLV to measure skill with, and no way to know whether a line we originate is right. This is Q-01 and it blocks 9 of 14 gates.
    2. Player-level entitlement. Snaps, routes and targets are what turn "a starter is out" into "the replacement costs 2.1 yards per carry". This is Q-02.
    3. A defensible depth answer. The league publishes no depth chart, so "2nd/3rd/4th string replacing a starter" must be reconstructed from roster status, participation, designations and actual playing time (S-06, FL-06).
    4. Point-in-time storage. Three filings a week and a dated event log, or the availability signals cannot be built at all (FL-09).

      5. What changed on 13 November 2026

      The league made one Week 10 game official after the last build. Two things follow from it, and both are on the site rather than in a commit message:

      1. The standings were one game stale. NYG and WAS each moved by exactly the settled 24–21 game. Both rows are refreshed, the league identities still close (136–136, 6291 points for and against), and the episode is recorded as FL-35 because the real defect is that no cumulative table on this site states when it was read.
      2. The site has a settled result for the first time. Every line this repository originated for that game is now scored against the official final score on the Settled Week 10 page. Our spread lost to a predictor that knows nothing about either team by 2.5 points; our total beat the archived price by 4.0 points, on a game whose total landed 0.93 points from the league mean — only 8 of the 136 verified games are that average. The page publishes the arithmetic rather than the flattering reading: detecting a 1.0-point difference at 80% power needs about 1,400 games, so one settled game is 0.07% of the evidence required.

        3. Nothing was appended to the weeks 1–9 backtest corpus. Suggestion S-26 asks for that file to be the pre-registered corpus with its hash published, so week 10 results go into a separate append-only file and the corpus stays byte-identical at sha256 f0dffce0…6f33.

        Two items in the register came out of the re-read and are waiting on you rather than on code: the refresh cadence for cumulative tables (Q-40, S-43) and a broadcaster disagreement on two Week 11 games (FL-36).

        What changed on 13 November 2026, later

        A settlement is not a forecast. The one graded Week 10 game was published after the fact, so it proves the arithmetic runs and nothing else. The thing a competition needs is a line published before the games it describes, and there was none — so this session built one.

        1. Thirteen Week 11 lines, pre-registered. For the games of 19–23 November: our spread and our total for each fixture, with the model inputs beside them, published with a decision timestamp of 2026-11-13T12:39Z and a SHA-256 over four artefacts — the lines file, the frozen weeks 1–9 corpus, the append-only settled-games file and the generator script. The full record is on the Week 11 lines page, and tools/grade_forward.py recomputes those hashes before it grades anything.
        2. The model was proved to be the same model, not a look-alike. The Week 11 generator re-implements the published ratings model, so before it writes anything it re-derives all fourteen already-published Week 10 lines from the frozen corpus and compares every cell. It matched 14 of 14, and tools/verify.py now re-runs that guard on every verification.
        3. The pre-registration was tested, not asserted. Editing one spread from 5.5 to 1.5 makes the grader report BROKEN, withhold the grade and exit non-zero. Restoring it returns intact.
        4. Two flags and two questions came out of a 102-row re-verification. All 32 standings rows, all 14 Week 10 fixtures and all 13 Week 11 fixtures were re-read against the live official pages on 13 November: 95 matches, 2 mismatches, 5 rows that say NOT CHECKED. The two mismatches are the Week 11 broadcasters — and they are now explained, not just flagged: the league's own announcement of 9 November matches this repository's transcription cell for cell, so the league moved the two broadcasts between 9 and 13 November (FL-36). Two new questions ask which official page is the record when the league changes a schedule after announcing it (Q-46) and when participation for the next week will exist (Q-47).
        5. Two suggestions were implemented. S-44 (auto-grade every originated line against the settled result) and S-45 (publish the required-sample number beside every performance figure) are now code, and the standings table carries the capture timestamp S-43 asked for.

          6. The honest finding on the lines themselves: our spreads sit about 3.5 points closer to a pick'em than the community archive's — the model's fitted home-field coefficient is 0.18 points, where the archive's widest lines are 14 and 16.5. The totals remain the weak half. And the sample-size arithmetic is unchanged and unforgiving: thirteen observations against a required ~1,400.
